# Map of Unknowns

Explorers' maps were most useful where they admitted blank space. Most summaries of a field describe what is known and hide the edges, which is exactly where research, opportunity and risk live. This skill draws the blank space on purpose.

## Categories

- **Settled:** known with high confidence. Keep brief; it frames the rest.
- **Open questions:** recognised by the field as unanswered.
- **Disputed:** answered differently by credible camps.
- **Unmeasured:** answerable in principle, but nobody has collected the data.
- **Hidden assumptions:** things everyone takes for granted that have never been tested.
- **Unknowable for now:** blocked by physics, ethics, cost or time horizon.

## For each unknown, record

- why it is still open (no data, hard to measure, no funding, incentives against, ethical limits, too new)
- what would close it (a study, a dataset, an instrument, a natural experiment, time)
- what it would change if closed (which decisions or beliefs depend on it)
- who is working on it, if anyone

## Procedure

1. Define the field or question and its boundary.
2. Survey: recent reviews, "open problems" lists, "limitations" and "future work" sections, and expert disagreements. Look up current work; open questions get answered.
3. Sort findings into the categories.
4. Hunt hidden assumptions by asking of each settled claim: what would have to be true for this to fail?
5. Rank unknowns by decision impact times tractability.

## Output

| Unknown | Category | Why open | What would close it | Impact if closed | Who is on it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Plus the top three unknowns worth attacking first, with a one-line plan each.

## Rules

- Do not present your own ignorance as the field's. Check before calling something unknown.
- Name the camps in disputes; do not average them.

## Handoffs

Hypothesis Lab to attack a tractable unknown; Claims Ledger for the settled part; Revelations 1:16 for information that exists but is being withheld.
