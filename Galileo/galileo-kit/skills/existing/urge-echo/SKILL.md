# Urge Echo

An agent is an echolocator turned inward and outward: an **urge** (drang) fires a **ping**, the world or the self sends a **return**, and the agent **adapts** its next output. Experience, behaviour and identity are read as the loop, not as a static trait.

## The loop

1. **Urge (drang)** - the forced pressure to act, with or without a real need. Split it:
   - *Need-urge*: hunger, safety, rest. Ends when the need ends.
   - *Surplus-urge*: continues after need is met (the soda sip when full, scrolling, wanting to be seen). This is where learning, hijacking and identity live.
   - Always separate **wanting** (drive, dopamine-like, cue-triggered) from **liking** (the actual reward). A gap between them marks a hijacked or conditioned urge.
2. **Ping** - the self-made signal sent out to get information: a message, a run into space, a trade, a post, a question, a movement. Every ping costs energy and reveals method.
3. **Return** - what comes back, including receivers the agent never sees (a neighbour, a cat, a satellite, an algorithm). Note who or what can return, and what is withheld.
4. **Forward model** - the agent's prediction of the return. Learning is the mismatch between predicted and actual return (efference copy / prediction error).
5. **Arbiter** - the part that picks which urge fires and which is refused. Mark where override happens and what it costs.
6. **Diversion** - surplus urge channelled into hobbies, skills, building, reading, thinking. Track what each diversion is diverted *from*.
7. **Zugzwang** - states where every available move worsens the position but standing still is not allowed. Flag where the urge forces a move anyway.

## Procedure

1. Name the agent and the time scale (second, day, career, era).
2. List its urges; tag need vs surplus and wanting vs liking.
3. Map pings and receivers; note unanswered pings and unseen receivers.
4. Estimate the forward model and where it keeps getting corrected or not.
5. Locate the arbiter and its override cost.
6. Trace diversions and their energy budget.
7. Mark zugzwang points and hijack points (cue-to-urge conditioning).
8. Report: dominant loop, weakest link, the hijack or diversion with the most leverage, and next likely ping.

## Applications

- **People**: habits, compulsions, attention, creative output, why a diversion persists after the need ends.
- **Football**: a call for the ball is a ping; pair with Hunter's Call to read intent before the pass.
- **AI agents**: urge = objective, ping = tool call or output, return = environment feedback, forward model = world model. Note the missing grounding: returns arrive as text, with no physical cost.
- **Markets and institutions**: urge = incentive, ping = signal or policy, return = price or reaction, arbiter = governance. Pair with Warpoker for leakage.
- **Meta**: the experienced part (the receiver of the return) is flagged as an open question, never asserted or denied.

## Rules

- Do not assume a drive is a need; test whether it persists after the need ends.
- Keep the felt part as the unexplained step, and label claims about it as hypothesis.
- Score each urge by cue, wanting, liking, cost and who answers the ping.
- Keep outputs short and dense unless asked to expand.
