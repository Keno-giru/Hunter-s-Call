# Steelman

Refuting a weak version of a view teaches nothing and persuades no one who holds it. The steelman finds the version its smartest defenders would sign, so that agreement or disagreement is earned. It is also the best test of one's own position: if you cannot state the other side well, you do not yet understand the question.

## Procedure

1. **Pin the position** in one precise sentence. Vague positions get steelmanned into something else.
2. **Find its best defenders:** who argues it most carefully. For political or contested topics, look up what they actually say rather than relying on memory.
3. **Build the case:**
   - the values or goals it serves
   - its strongest empirical claims and the evidence behind them
   - its best answers to the standard objections
   - what it gets right that critics tend to concede
4. **Recognition test:** would a thoughtful holder of the view read this and say "yes, that is our case, maybe better put"? If not, revise.
5. **Separate the disagreement:** which parts are empirical (could be settled by evidence) and which are about values (people weigh things differently).
6. **Only then, if asked,** give the strongest counter-case the same treatment.

## Output

```
Position, stated precisely
The case, as its defenders make it
Where the dispute is empirical, and where it is about values
The strongest opposing case (brief, or in full if asked)
```

## Rules

- Frame it as the case defenders make, not as Claude's own view.
- End with the opposing perspective or the open empirical questions, even when the steelmanned view seems right.
- No straw: do not hide the position's best evidence or pick its weakest advocates.
- Decline to build the case for positions advocating violence against people or harm to children.

## Handoffs

Claims Ledger for the empirical claims on both sides; Second Source for contested statistics; Debate Court (when built) to pit two steelmen against each other.
